\section*{JOB MARKET PAPER}
``Reassessed \& Repossessed: Who Bears a Reassessment Shock?'', Sole-Authored
\begin{list}{}{\setlength{\leftmargin}{0.5in}\setlength{\rightmargin}{0.5in}\setlength{\topsep}{4pt}}
\item[] \textbf{Abstract:} Most U.S.\ homes are assessed on mandated cycles of three years or longer. Appreciation goes untaxed between assessments and arrives as a discrete increase in the tax bill in the assessment year, which escrow passes through to the monthly mortgage payment. I study eight countywide assessments in the five largest North Carolina counties between 2011 and 2019, comparing each with counties that assess every year by synthetic difference in differences, and link each home's tax bill to its recorded deeds and foreclosure filings. An assessment raises a home's quarterly probability of a foreclosure filing by 31 percent, about 610 additional filings over two years in the median county. The additional filings decline steadily with home value, from 0.70 per 100 homes in the cheapest quartile to 0.37 in the most expensive. Where bills can be measured, a 10 percent larger bill increase roughly doubles the filing probability of the cheapest homes and leaves that of the most expensive unchanged. Long assessment cycles concentrate payment shocks, and the foreclosure filings that follow, on the owners least able to absorb them.
\end{list}
